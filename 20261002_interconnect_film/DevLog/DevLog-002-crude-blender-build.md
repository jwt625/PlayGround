## How it is built

- Timeline: scene i occupies frames 1 + i*300 ... (i+1)*300; S7 (disclaimer) is frames 1801-1860. Beat times in the scripts are seconds within a scene, matching DevLog-001 Section 4.
- One camera with a Track-To target; each shot keys position/target/lens and ends with a constant-interpolation key, so shot boundaries are hard cuts (continuous moves inside a shot).
- Cast reused across scenes (Gary, Manager, shotgun, AOC-bundle whip, plus per-scene people) so bullet holes persist; objects are shown/hidden per scene by keyed visibility. Hole recovery: after each hit the hole radius steps down (x0.9 every 1.5 s, floor 30%) and never reaches zero.
- Text, all in Blender (no ffmpeg drawtext needed): narration chunks (white, lower third, 1-3 words each, timed by word count within each narration window), big gag words (yellow, center), technical labels (cyan, top-left), source cards (small gray, bottom-left), `[FX: ...]` notes (yellow, top-left), a time code `S# m:ss` (bottom-right) for feedback by timestamp, and in-world white billboard labels. Overlay text sits 0.8 m in front of the camera, scaled to hold a constant screen size, so geometry never occludes it.
- Render: EEVEE (Blender 4.2.3, `BLENDER_EEVEE_NEXT`), 12 samples, Standard view transform, 720x900. Roughly 1 frame/s end to end on this Mac for v0.1 (about 25-30 min for 1860 frames; the v0 scenes were cheaper).

## What is still crude on purpose

Primitive characters (no facial animation beyond the Manager's brows and mouth), no particles/glow/smoke/steam (labeled `[FX: ...]`), no motion blur, egg frying is stacked discs with scale growth (the real shader is the planned riskiest look), CM300 and other equipment are box models, bullet holes are black through-cylinders, packages are boxes with ball/pad grids.

## Verification notes
