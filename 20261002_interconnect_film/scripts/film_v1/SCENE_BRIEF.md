# Scene assembly brief (v1.0)

Audience: the agent assembling one scene of the film from the asset library. Date: 2026-10-02.

## Goal

Build ONE scene of the approved storyboard as its own Blender file, using the accurate assets from `assets/components/` (indexed in `assets/INDEX.md`, details in each asset's `.json` and in the category devlogs `DevLog/v1/DevLog-003-<cat>.md`), with the same timing, camera moves, captions and narration text as the crude v0.2 version, so that Wentao can review the modeling in context. Storyboard: `DevLog/DevLog-001-story-scenes-assets-proposal.md` Section 4 (rev 5, one table per scene) and Section 5 (narration). The crude reference implementation of every scene is `scripts/build_crude_film.py` (functions s1..s6): copy its timing, camera positions (scaled to real size), caption/label/card texts and FX notes. v0.2 video for reference: `outputs/crude_v0_2_20261002.mp4`.

## Framework (read first)

- `scripts/film_v1/asm.py` (assembly framework: `new_scene`, `rig`, `append`, `clone`, `unique_material`, `place`, `set_prop`, `key_prop`, `key_loc`, `play` (NLA action strips), `walk`, `attach`, `fx`, `show` (collection visibility windows), captions `cap/big/lab/card/fxn/narr/wl`, `shot`, `finalize`) and `scripts/film_v1/test_asm.py` (a working smoke test: Gary + Manager + rack pair).
- `scripts/blender_lib.py` is reused for camera shots, overlay text and visibility windows (scene index 0). `scripts/tex_gen.py` makes the eye / BER, whiteboard graph and ring spectrum image sequences exactly as in the crude film (see the crude `eye_material`, graph and spectrum code in `build_crude_film.py`).
- One .blend per scene, scene-local time: frame = 1 + round(t * 30), 300 frames (10 s), 30 fps, 1080x1350 (4:5). Output: `scenes/v1/sNN_<name>.blend`; build script: `scripts/film_v1/sNN_<name>.py`, runnable as `/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b --python scripts/film_v1/sNN_<name>.py -- scenes/v1/sNN_<name>.blend`.
- Characters: `characters/gary`, `characters/manager`, `characters/npc_*` (append with `actions=True`); actions `ACT_<asset>_<name>`; expressions via root properties `p_expr_<name>`, `p_anger`, `p_flush`; bullet holes via `p_hole_k_radius` (0..1; the storyboard rule: after each hit a hole shrinks by x0.9 every 1.5 s down to a floor of 0.3, never zero; hole k is created in scene k of the film, so earlier holes are already present at partially healed radii: S1 hole 1 appears at its bang; in S2 hole 1 starts at 0.9^(10/1.5) ~ 0.5 of a radius that was 1.0 when shot, never below 0.3; in later scenes use the schedule `r = max(0.3, 0.9 ** ((T_now - T_shot) / 1.5))` with film times T_shot: hole 1 at 9.2 s, hole 2 at 19.2 s (S2 t 9.2), head hole at 28.95 s (S3 t 8.95), hole 4 at 38.9 s (S4 t 8.9), hole 5 at 49.1 s (S5 t 9.1). Gary's holes are listed in his JSON.) Manager shotgun: `props/shotgun` attached to the Manager's `HOOK_gun_grip_R` (see the Manager JSON), muzzle effects from `materials_vfx/vfx_particles_and_effects` via `asm.fx(...)`.
- Materials library: `materials_vfx/materials_vfx_library.blend` (egg-fry shader, heat-wave shader, clay and PBR). Lighting rigs `materials_vfx/lighting_and_world` via `asm.rig(...)`.
- Real scale: models are real size (1 unit = 1 m); human scale for characters. Where the storyboard needs macro views of tiny hardware (S4, S5), keep characters at human size and scale up the hardware group by a documented factor so the framing matches the storyboard; document the factor and why.
- Image-sequence screens: plug the sequences into the asset screen materials (see `DevLog/v1/DevLog-003-lab_office.md`: set `img.source='SEQUENCE'`, frame_duration, frame_start, set `SCREEN_FAC` to 1).
- Performance: scenes must render at about 1-5 s per frame at 1080x1350 (EEVEE Next, `standard` preset, 24 samples; measured 1.7 s for a light scene). Hide whatever the camera does not see (collection visibility windows via `asm.show`), use instancing or low-detail variants for repeated parts, and measure a few frames. If a scene is slower than 8 s per frame, report which assets cost the most.

## Process rules

- You own only: `scripts/film_v1/sNN_*.py` (plus optional `sNN_helpers.py`), `scenes/v1/sNN_*.blend`, `DevLog/v1/DevLog-003-scene-sNN.md`, and generated textures under `assets/generated_textures/v1/sNN/`. Do not edit `asm.py`, `blender_lib.py`, `tex_gen.py`, asset files, other scenes, or the crude scripts; if the framework needs a change, work around it in your own module and record the needed change in your devlog.
- No git, no commits. No emoji anywhere. ISO dates. Use python (not macOS sed) for text edits.
- Blender 4.2.3 at `/Applications/Blender.app`, headless, one process at a time, quit when done. Scratch files only in your session scratchpad (given in your prompt). Disk is tight (about 12 GiB free, shared): write test stills at 540x675 or smaller; keep each scene blend under 150 MB; do not render full sequences (the coordinator renders).
- Be token-economical (a shared usage window): first make the scene complete and correct in timing/camera/captions with the main assets, check a contact sheet of about 8 stills spanning the 10 s, fix the biggest problems, then refine. After each milestone append a timestamped line to your devlog. If you are about to run out of budget, make your last devlog entry an exact list of what is done, partial and not started.
- Accuracy: do not invent facts. Every on-screen number keeps the source tag from the crude cards.
- Final message: a report with the files, how to build/render, scale factors used, per-frame render cost you measured, which assets were used and any asset problems found (with names and what is wrong), deviations from the storyboard, and open questions.
