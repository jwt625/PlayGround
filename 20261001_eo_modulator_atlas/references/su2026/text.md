# **High Efficiency and High Speed Electro-Optical Modulator Based on Hybrid Calcium Titanate and Lithium Niobate**

**Wenqu Su1,2, Guanyu Chen1,2,3,\* , Tao Zhu1,2 and Hua Yu1,2**

*Key Laboratory of Optoelectronic Technology & Systems Ministry of Education, Chongqing University, Chongqing 400044, China College of Optoelectronic Engineering, Chongqing University, Chongqing 400044, China Institute of Advanced Interdisciplinary Studies, Chongqing University, Chongqing 400044, China \*gychen@cqu.edu.cn*

**Abstract:** We demonstrate a hybrid calcium titanate and thin film lithium niobate electro-optical modulator with 1.3 efficiency, > 110 GHz bandwidth and 0.86 dB insertion loss. © 2026 The Author(s) ! "#!

#### **1. Introduction**

The relentless surge in global data traffic calls for higher-performance electro-optic (EO) modulators to support nextgeneration optical communication systems [\[1\]](#page-2-0). Thin-film lithium niobate (TFLN) has emerged as an attractive platform owing to its strong EO effect [\[2-4\]](#page-2-1). However, TFLN modulators face an inherent trade-off among modulation efficiency, device footprint, and bandwidth. Enhancing efficiency typically requires longer devices, which reduces integration density and degrades high-frequency performance [\[5-7\]](#page-2-2).

To address this bottleneck, we introduce and experimentally demonstrate a novel TFLN Mach–Zehnder modulator (MZM) that employs a high-permittivity (high-k) calcium titanate (CaTiO3) functional cladding. A thin CaTiO3 film, an oxide perovskite material with a dielectric constant of up to 170, is integrated onto a TFLN ridge waveguide. Its exceptional dielectric properties are utilized to reshape the electric field distribution, allowing it to concentrate more effectively within the waveguide core. This design theoretically enhances the EO overlap factor and thereby improves modulation efficiency without increasing the device length. Experimentally, the proposed modulator achieves an efficiency of 1.3 and an ultra-wide EO bandwidth exceeding 110 GHz. These results not only validate the structural and high-frequency stability of CaTiO3 as a functional cladding, but also establish a new and practical pathway toward EO modulators with ultra-compact footprints, ultra-high bandwidth, and outstanding efficiency. ! ## **2. Device Design and Fabrication**

Figures 1(a) and 1(b) show the schematic and cross-sectional view of the hybrid CaTiO3/LN MZM, respectively. The device adopts a ground–signal–ground (GSG) electrode structure in a push–pull configuration to reduce the half-wave voltage (Vπ. In this design, a 300-nm-thick CaTiO3 film is deposited by plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) to cover the 400 nm thick gold (Au) electrodes.

A qualitative comparison of the simulated electric field distributions is presented in Figs. 1(c) and (d). Figure 1(c) depicts the field in a conventional device, where a significant portion of the microwave field leaks into the surrounding substrate and air. In contrast, Fig. 1(d) illustrates our CaTiO3-assisted design, showcasing a remarkable concentration of the electric field, tightly confined within the lithium niobate waveguide core. To precisely quantify this enhancement, we analyzed the electric field profile across the waveguide region, as plotted in Fig. 1(e). The simulation reveals that the peak electric field strength experienced by the optical mode is dramatically boosted from 1.22 kV cm-<sup>1</sup> in the conventional structure to 2.66 kV cm-1 with the CaTiO3 cladding. This significant improvement is attributed to the high dielectric constant of the CaTiO3 layer, which effectively focuses the microwave electric field lines into the active region. Consequently, this enhanced field confinement leads to a much stronger EO interaction, laying the foundation for achieving superior modulation efficiency.

#### **3. Device Characterization and Discussion**

Fabrication was performed on a commercial X-cut TFLN-on-insulator (LNOI) wafer with a 400 nm LN film. Ridge waveguides were defined by a 200 nm partial etch using Ar plasma. Ti/Au (20/400 nm) electrodes were patterned by e-beam evaporation and lift-off. A 300 nm CaTiO3 film was then deposited by PECVD. Finally, contact windows were opened over the electrode pads using laser lithography followed by a timed wet etch of the CaTiO3 in a buffered oxide etch (BOE) solution for radio frequency (RF) measurements.

The devices were realized on a 4-inch TFLN wafer, as shown in Fig. 2(a). Figure 2(b) shows a top-view optical microscope image of the fully fabricated device. Two gratings were incorporated at both ends of the device to facilitate light coupling into and out of the chip for measurement. The TE0 mode light was coupled to the input grating, which was equally divided into two beams through the multi-mode interference (MMI) coupler. The measured total insertion loss of the modulator is 11.81 dB, while the on-chip propagation loss is 0.86 dB at 1550 nm, with each grating coupler contributing 5.45 dB of loss. Figure 2(c) shows a microscopic view of the electrode pad with CaTiO3 removed. The scanning electron microscope (SEM) image in Fig. 2(d) shows the cross-section of this structure. The sidewall roughness plays an important role in inducing scattering loss. The 3D AFM scan of the fabricated hybrid CaTiO3/LN waveguide is shown in Fig. 2(e). The sidewall angle of 64.37° and the etch depth of 200 nm are extracted from the line profile of the ridge waveguide, as shown in Fig. 2(f). A 3D image of the sidewall surface is shown in Fig. 2(g). The root-mean-squared (RMS) roughness of the sidewalls of the hybrid CaTiO3/LN waveguides is 0.595 nm in a 0.75 by-1.5-μm AFM scan area.

In order to quantify the effect of CaTiO3 cladding on the optical properties of the device, we measured the transmission spectrum of the microring resonator. As shown in Fig. 3(a), the microring with air cladding has a full width at half maximum (FWHM) of 13 pm and the corresponding Q factor is 1.3 × 105 . In contrast, the microring with CaTiO3 cladding has a FWHM of 8 pm, corresponding to a load quality (Q) factor of up to 2.0 × 105 , as shown in Fig. 3(b). The results show that the CaTiO3 cladding leads to a Q factor enhancement of more than 50% by improving the optical mode confinement and passivating the waveguide surface. A higher Q factor means lower loss. The propagation loss for the CaTiO3 cladding waveguide was calculated to be 8.2 dB/cm.

A 100-kHz sawtooth electric signal was applied to the electrodes to measure the half-wave voltage. All the measured devices featured a 1 mm length modulation region. As shown in Fig.3(c), the hybrid CaTiO3/LN modulator exhibited a half-wave voltage length product as low as 1.3 . ! "#!

For bandwidth measurements, a GSG RF probe was employed to administer microwave signals to the electrodes. Microwave transmission S21 and reflection S11 were gauged using a light wave component analyzer (LCA). The measured results show that the 3 dB EO bandwidth (S21) exceeds the 110 GHz measurement limit of the instrument at the wavelength of 1550 nm. Concurrently, the electrical reflection (S11) remained below -8 dB across the entire band, indicating excellent impedance matching. This proves that the CaTiO3 cladding provides a stable electromagnetic environment for high-speed operation without introducing additional high-frequency loss.

### **4. Conclusions**

We demonstrate a high-performance modulator on a hybrid CaTiO3/TFLN integrated platform. Our device simultaneously achieves an ultra-high modulation efficiency (VπL) of 1.3 , a 3 dB EO bandwidth exceeding 110 GHz, and a low insertion loss of 0.86 dB. By employing foundry-compatible processes, our approach can be readily integrated with germanium silicon photodetectors, facilitating the realization of more complex on-chip photonic circuits. ! "#!
